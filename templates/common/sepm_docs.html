Attendance Management System (AMS)

Department of Computer Science & Engineering • Bachelor of Technology (6th Semester)

1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

The Attendance Management System (AMS) is a comprehensive web-based institutional application designed to digitize, streamline, and secure the tracking of student attendance across undergraduate courses.

1.1 Problem Statement

Conventional paper-based registers in higher educational institutions suffer from notable operational bottlenecks:

  • Instructional Time Loss: Faculty spend 5 to 10 minutes of every 60-minute lecture manually calling out names.
  • Proxy Attendance & Human Errors: Manual roll-call registers are prone to proxy marking and arithmetic errors when compiling percentages.
  • Delayed Defaulter Identification: Semester attendance shortages (<75%) are often identified late in the semester, leaving students unable to rectify their attendance.
  • Data Vulnerability: Physical registers can be lost, damaged, or tampered with.

1.2 Project Objectives

  1. Provide a centralized SQLite relational database for tamper-resistant attendance logging.
  2. Equip faculty with an optimized marker interface featuring quick "Mark All" toggles, live counters, and duplicate session safeguards.
  3. Provide students with a transparent portal featuring real-time percentage tracking and statutory shortage warnings.
  4. Enable administrators to manage users, subjects, assignments, and audit reports seamlessly.

3. Software Requirement Specification (SRS - IEEE 830)

3.1 Functional Requirements (FR)

  • FR-01 (Authentication): System must authenticate users via username/email and securely hashed passwords using PBKDF2/SHA-256.
  • FR-02 (RBAC): Strict Role-Based Access Control enforcing Admin, Faculty, and Student boundary separation.
  • FR-03 (Admin Operations): Create, read, update, deactivate, and delete user and subject profiles.
  • FR-04 (Faculty Marking): Record single-session attendance with date, slot, topic, and Present/Absent states.
  • FR-05 (Duplicate Safeguard): Prevent recording multiple attendance sessions for the same subject, date, and slot.
  • FR-06 (Student Visibility): Students can view their overall percentage, subject-wise percentages, and historical logs in read-only mode.
  • FR-07 (Statutory Alert): Automatically calculate and display shortage warning badges whenever attendance drops below 75.0%.
  • FR-08 (Data Export): Generate compliant CSV registers for external record preservation.

3.2 Non-Functional Requirements (NFR)

  • Security: No plain-text passwords stored; HTTP session cookies signed with server-side secret key; protection against CSRF and role escalation.
  • Performance: Sub-second response times for attendance roster loading and submission (processed in <100ms on SQLite).
  • Reliability: Relational integrity enforced via SQLite `PRAGMA foreign_keys = ON;` and atomic ACID transactions.
  • Usability: Responsive interface compatible with mobile devices, laptops, and lab desktop screens.

8. Project Management: Estimation & Scheduling

8.1 COCOMO (Constructive Cost Model) Estimation

Using Intermediate COCOMO for an Organic mode software project:

  • Estimated Size: 2.8 KLOC (2,800 lines of Python, HTML, CSS, JS)
  • Effort Formula: \( E = a_b \times (\text{KLOC})^{b_b} = 2.4 \times (2.8)^{1.05} \approx \mathbf{7.12\text{ Person-Months}} \)
  • Development Time: \( D = c_b \times (E)^{d_b} = 2.5 \times (7.12)^{0.38} \approx \mathbf{5.27\text{ Months}} \) (academic term equivalent)

8.2 Risk Management Plan (RMMM)

Identified Risk Probability / Impact Mitigation Strategy

9. Viva Voce Questions & Answers Guide

Quick-reference answers to common questions asked by university external examiners during evaluation:

Q1: Why did you choose SQLite over MySQL or PostgreSQL for this SEPM project?
Answer: SQLite is a self-contained, serverless, zero-configuration transactional database engine. It avoids external server daemon dependencies, simplifies project portability for college evaluation, and natively supports ACID transactions and foreign key constraints without overhead.
Q2: How does the system prevent accidental duplicate attendance marking?
Answer: At the database level, a unique composite index exists on (subject_id, session_date, session_slot) in the attendance_sessions table. At the application layer in models.py, check_existing_session() is executed before any insertion, warning the faculty and offering a safe "Edit Session" pathway.
Q3: How are passwords secured against database breach?
Answer: Passwords are never stored in plain text. We utilize Werkzeug's cryptographic hashing implementation (generate_password_hash), which applies a cryptographically secure random salt and PBKDF2 with SHA-256 key stretching, rendering rainbow table and brute-force attacks computationally infeasible.
Q4: How is Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) enforced?
Answer: RBAC is enforced through Python function decorators (@login_required and @role_required('admin')) wrapping Flask route controllers. Even if a student manually modifies client-side HTML or enters an administrative URL directly into the address bar, the decorator intercepts the request, verifies the server-side session token, and issues an immediate 403 Forbidden redirect.
Q5: What formula calculates the required classes to reach 75%?
Answer: Let \( A \) be classes attended, \( C \) classes conducted, and \( x \) consecutive classes needed. We solve: \( \frac{A + x}{C + x} \ge 0.75 \implies x \ge \frac{0.75 \cdot C - A}{1 - 0.75} \). This mathematical guidance is dynamically computed and displayed on the student's subject cards.
